## 2. De-duplication

Profiling found **2,865,776 rows but only 2,695,890 distinct case numbers**. There were two causes:

1. The FY2023 Q2 file repeats ~90k cases from FY2023 Q1 (identical rows).
2. When an employer withdraws a certified LCA, DOL re-publishes the case in a later file with status `Certified - Withdrawn`. We saw this in up to 3 files per case.

**Rule:** one row per case. The **status comes from the latest file** (the most up-to-date outcome). The **decision date comes from the earliest record** (when the case was first decided, which sets its fiscal year).
**Where:** `sql/01_clean_base.sql`, STEP A.

## 3. Which cases count

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **Final status = `Certified` only** | `Denied` and `Withdrawn` cases never became valid LCAs. `Certified - Withdrawn` (154,286 H-1B cases) were certified but later withdrawn by the employer, so they don't represent a live job. All statuses stay in `lca_all` for comparison. |
| **Visa class = `H-1B` only** | E-3 (Australia, 42,821 certified) and H-1B1 (Chile/Singapore, 15,306) use the same form but are different visas with no lottery. They are excluded from `lca_certified` but kept in `lca_all`. |
| **Unit of analysis = one LCA (case)** | We count cases, not `total_worker_positions`. One LCA can cover several positions, but large position counts usually mean "we might hire up to N", not N hires. Counting cases is the conservative choice. Power BI exposes both. |

## 4. Wages

| Decision | Why | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Use `WAGE_RATE_OF_PAY_FROM`** (the bottom of the offered range) | It is the guaranteed minimum. **32.0%** of certified LCAs give a range (31.5% of data roles), and when they do the top is a median **1.22×** the bottom. Medians from the FROM value are therefore conservative. | `01_clean_base.sql` `annual_wage` |
| **Annualize with Year×1, Month×12, Bi-Weekly×26, Week×52, Hour×2080** | 2080 = 40 h × 52 weeks, the standard full-time conversion. | same |
| **Salary statistics use full-time positions only** | Annualizing a part-time hourly rate at 2080 h would overstate pay. 38,500 part-time certified LCAs are kept in the data but excluded from salary stats. | `wage_ok_for_stats` in `03_clean_roles_final.sql` |
| **Flag outliers; never "fix" them** | We don't know the true value, and correcting would mean inventing data. Flagged rows stay in the tables (they still count as sponsorship) but are excluded from salary stats. | `flag_*` columns |
| ↳ `flag_unit_suspect` (2,285) | The number doesn't fit its unit: ≥ $500/hour, ≥ $20k/week, ≥ $40k/bi-weekly, ≥ $85k/month, or < $1,000/year. **Evidence:** before this rule, the median "weekly" wage annualized to **$4.56M**. Those are annual salaries with the wrong box ticked. | |
| ↳ `flag_wage_too_low` (< $30k annualized, 524) / `flag_wage_too_high` (> $1M, 2,419) | Implausible for a full-time professional H-1B role. The categories overlap with the unit flag. | |
| ↳ `flag_below_prevailing` (4) | The law requires offered ≥ prevailing wage. Below 95% of it means a data error. | |
| **Result: 98.3% of certified LCAs are usable for salary stats** | 2,374,126 of 2,415,525. | validation report §5 |
| **The offered wage is base salary only** | LCAs don't include bonus or equity. That's why banks look cheap: Goldman's Level I "Analyst" offers have a $67k median base. Big-tech totals are also understated. Say "offered base wage" in interviews. | — |

## 5. Geography

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| **NYC = worksite in the five borough counties** (New York, Kings, Queens, Bronx, Richmond, with or without the word "COUNTY"), **or** a NYC city/neighborhood name when the county is blank | The county is the most reliable field. The city fallback catches rows like `NEW YORK` with a blank county (4,429 rows). Jersey City, Long Island, and Westchester are **not** NYC: commuters may disagree, but the five boroughs is the standard definition. |
| **Primary worksite only** | Each LCA lists one main worksite. Extra worksites live in a separate DOL "Worksites" appendix that this project doesn't load. Multi-site LCAs (mostly IT staffing) are counted once, at their first worksite. |
| **`is_nyc` blank-state rows → FALSE** | A NULL state made the boolean NULL (SQL three-valued logic). We found 1,544 such rows during validation and fixed them with `coalesce(..., false)`. |

## 6. Employer names

Goal: the same company should count as one employer, but **we don't merge companies that are actually different**.

| Layer | Rule | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Raw | — | 182,508 distinct spellings |
| 1. Text normalization | Uppercase, `&`→`AND`, punctuation→space, drop a leading `THE`, repeatedly strip trailing legal suffixes (`INC, LLC, LLP, LP, LTD, CORP, CORPORATION, CO, COMPANY, PLC, PC, PLLC, NA, USA, US, GROUP, HOLDINGS, AND`) | 144,708 |
| 2. Spacing merge | Names equal after removing *all* spaces (`JP MORGAN` = `JPMORGAN`) → most frequent spelling | 143,616 (−1,092 names) |
| 3. FEIN merge | FY2024+ files include the employer's federal tax ID. Names whose dominant FEIN (≥ 80% of their rows) is the same → one name. **Universities, colleges, schools, states, counties and cities are exempt**, because a state university system shares one FEIN across separate campuses (we caught UMass Amherst being merged into UMass Chan Medical School). | 139,671 (−3,945 names) |
| 4. Parent roll-up (manual) | A short, explicit list of well-known multi-entity employers: Amazon (25 legal entities), Google, Meta, JPMorgan, Citi, American Express, Visa, Discover, CVS (incl. Caremark) and others. | used in `employer_parent` |

- Why `AND` is in the suffix list: `JPMorgan Chase & Co.` → `JPMORGAN CHASE AND CO` → after stripping `CO` → `JPMORGAN CHASE AND`. Stripping a trailing `AND` fixes it.
- Why Aetna is **not** rolled into CVS (its owner): a job seeker applies to Aetna as Aetna.
- Every merge is in `outputs/employer_merge_log.csv` (5,048 entries: 1,058 spacing, 3,990 FEIN; one row per original-name → final-name pair). Skim it: the large merges are rebrands (Anthem→Elevance, L&T Infotech→LTIMindtree, Modis→Akkodis) and DBA names.
- **Known limitation:** subsidiaries with different FEINs and no manual rule (e.g. Fidelity has FMR, Fidelity Technology, and Fidelity Brokerage) stay separate. The analysis ranks by `employer_parent`, so those would split their counts.

## 7. Role families

Rules live in `sql/03_clean_roles_final.sql`. They are evaluated **in order; the first match wins**, and the rule that fired is stored in `role_rule` so any row can be traced.

| Order | Rule | Family | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | Exclusions | Other | Product/project/program **manager**, DBA, data **center**, **ML/AI engineer**, clinical data manager, quantitative analyst/researcher, statistical programmer, data entry |
| R1 | Manager word + data word | Analytics Manager | "Senior Manager, Data Science", "Director of Analytics", "VP, Data Analytics" |
| R2 | Scientist words | Data Scientist | data scien*, applied scien*, ML scientist, decision scientist, statistician |
| R3 | Engineer words | Data Engineer | data engineer, big data, ETL, data warehouse, data architect, analytics engineer, database developer |
| R4 | Analyst words | Data Analyst | data analy*, statistical analyst, reporting/insights/product analyst, analytics analyst |
| R5 | Business/BI words | Business/BI Analyst | business analyst, business systems analyst, BI, Power BI, Tableau, "Business Intelligence Engineer" |
| R6 | **SOC fallback**, only for generic titles ("Analyst", "Senior Associate", "Consultant II") | per SOC | Bank rank titles at Goldman/Morgan Stanley filed under SOC 15-2051 Data Scientists |

**Why the order matters:** R0 runs first so "Product Manager, Analytics" never becomes an Analytics Manager. R4 runs before R5 so "Business Data Analyst" counts as a Data Analyst.

**Deliberate exclusions** (titles a reasonable person could put in a data family):
- **ML engineers:** these are software-engineering roles. Including them would inflate Data Scientist with a different job.
- **Quant analysts and researchers:** a separate finance career track, and a large group in NYC.
- **Statistical programmers:** clinical-trial SAS programming.
- **Operations-research and market-research analysts** with non-data titles: only 27% of SOC 15-2031 rows are captured.

**Coverage** (validation report §9): 78% of SOC 15-2051.00 "Data Scientists" LCAs are classified as a data role. Most of the rest are ML engineers, product managers, and software engineers, which we excluded on purpose (§10 lists them).

**Accuracy spot-check.** I manually reviewed 20 deterministic-random rows per family (§11 of the validation report):

| Family | Correct | Doubtful |
|---|---|---|
| Data Analyst | 19/20 | "Data Analytics Architect L2" (closer to a data engineer) |
| Business/BI Analyst | 20/20 | note: includes IT "Business Systems Analysts" by design |
| Data Scientist | 19/20 | "Data Scientist, Software Developer, IT Specialist" (a catch-all title) |
| Data Engineer | 20/20 | |
| Analytics Manager | 20/20 | |
| Other (should be non-data) | 19/20 | an EY "Operations Research Analyst" is arguably a data role |

≈ **97–98% precision** on the sample. With n=20 per family this is indicative, not a measured error rate.

## 8. "Tech" vs "non-tech"

- **Tech = NAICS 51** (Information), **5415** (Computer Systems Design: IT services and consulting), **334** (computer and electronic hardware), **OR a big-tech parent** (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Apple, Netflix, NVIDIA, Salesforce, Oracle, IBM, Uber, TikTok/ByteDance).
- **Why the parent override:** Amazon files under retail NAICS 4541/4551. Without the override, "non-tech" would be led by Amazon, which would make the headline finding wrong.
- **Known gray zone:** online retailers (Wayfair, Chewy, eBay, Etsy), fintechs (PayPal) and delivery apps (Grubhub) are "non-tech" by NAICS even though many people would call them tech. They are a small share and are listed openly in the hidden-sponsor tables.
- **Hidden sponsors** additionally exclude management consulting (5416), accounting/Big 4 (5412) and staffing (5613). Their business is advising or placing people, and we want companies whose *own* business (a bank, insurer, retailer, or hospital) hires data people.
- "Consistent" means **at least N data LCAs in each of FY2022–FY2025** (N = 5 nationally, 2 in NYC).

## 9. Entry-level signal

DOL prevailing-wage **Levels I and II** are the tiers employers use for positions needing less experience. We treat Level I/II as "realistic for new grads".
**Caveat:** the level describes the *job*, as the employer classified it. It doesn't prove the person hired was a new grad, and employers have a financial incentive to classify lower.
